On 13 March 2018, trains stopped running between [Angoulême](gare:angouleme) and [Saillat-Chassenon](gare:saillat-chassenon), on the [line from Limoges-Bénédictins to Angoulême](ligne:610000)[^france3-2018]. Faults in the track geometry meant trains could no longer run safely. The next day, SNCF announced a temporary closure of three weeks, as a member of parliament for the Charente recalled in a question to the government in 2023[^qe8999]. More than eight years later, the section has not reopened. Coaches cover the journey, and trains from Limoges terminate at Saillat.

The AngouLim association is calling for the whole line to reopen. As of 22 November 2026, the preliminary study has been delivered, but funding for the reopening works has not been decided.

## A line of the Compagnie des Charentes

The line began as a concession granted by the State to the Compagnie des chemins de fer des Charentes: the agreement was signed on 18 July 1868 and the line declared of public utility on 26 July[^wikipedia]. The chosen route runs through [La Rochefoucauld](gare:la-rochefoucauld), then follows the Vienne valley through [Chabanais](gare:chabanais), [Saint-Junien](gare:st-junien) and [Aixe-sur-Vienne](gare:aixe-sur-vienne). The line opened on 26 April 1875; the connection to [Limoges-Bénédictins](gare:limoges-benedictins) station followed a year later[^wikipedia].

The Charentes company did not keep its line for long. The State bought it (agreement of 31 March 1877, law of 18 May 1878), and the line passed to the State railways administration. An agreement of 1883 transferred it to the Paris-Orléans company, which ran it until SNCF was created in 1938[^wikipedia].

The line is 117 kilometres long, single track and not electrified; its gradients do not exceed 20 per thousand[^wikipedia][^qe8999]. It crosses the Tardoire, the Charente, the Gorre and the Vienne, the last on a 100 metre viaduct, and goes underground at Verneuil and at Montjovis, on the edge of Limoges[^wikipedia]. For more than half a century a night train used it: a Geneva - La Rochelle express from 1938, replaced in 1957 by a Saint-Gervais-les-Bains - La Rochelle service that ended up running only at weekends, before it was withdrawn in 1990[^wikipedia].

## Years of wear

Before the closure, the line carried 21 regional trains a day and about 80,000 passengers a year, according to the ministry's answer to a written question in 2024[^qe15914]. Several stations on the Charente section, such as Fontafie, Taponnat and Magnac-Touvre, had already closed[^wikipedia]. In summer 2016, 5,000 sleepers had to be replaced as a matter of urgency[^wikipedia]; at the end of 2017, further emergency works cost 3.12 million euros, 1.06 million of it paid by the State[^qe15914].

Those works did not prevent the closure of March 2018. Replacement coaches took over; since 15 December 2019 they have taken about two hours for the journey[^wikipedia]. Freight had already gone in the 2010s, according to the member of parliament René Pilato, who counted in 2024 about ten private sidings still in place along the line[^qe15914].

## Limoges - Saillat, the half that still runs

In the east, the line has not closed. Regional trains on line 18 of the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region still run between Limoges and Saillat-Chassenon, but that track is wearing out too. In 2019, a study by the consultancy Systra estimated that this section would last only two to five more years without work[^wikipedia]. To avoid a 40 km/h speed limit in 2024, the State and the Region scheduled works for 2023[^qe8999]. From September to December 2023, 10.8 kilometres of track were renewed between Aixe-sur-Vienne and Limoges, along with two engineering structures and two level crossings, for 9.92 million euros[^region]. The government, for its part, gives 10 million, 4.7 million of it from the State[^qo433].

## What bringing the trains back would cost

The estimates have changed a great deal in seven years. In March 2019, Systra put the reopening at 100 to 125 million euros[^wikipedia]. Preliminary studies, funded by the recovery plan, began in 2021[^qe8999]. Their first results, in November 2023, gave 242 million euros, works from 2027 and a target reopening around 2030, with a first phase of 35 million shared between the State (40%), the Region (40%) and other local authorities (20%)[^wikipedia].

SNCF Réseau's preliminary study, delivered at the end of June 2025, changed the scale: 360 million euros to reopen the whole line, 260 million of it for the closed section and 100 million to bring Limoges - Saillat up to standard[^qo433][^france3-2025]. Those 360 million include the section still open, which the 242 million of 2023 did not: the two figures cannot be compared directly.

On funding, the 2015-2022 State-Region plan contract provided 6.4 million euros from the State for emergency works and studies. The 2023-2027 contract sets aside 34 million euros for studies and renewal of the line, 13.6 million of it from the State[^qo433].

## AngouLim and the campaign

In December 2018, about a hundred local councillors and railway workers demonstrated in the Haute-Vienne against a permanent closure[^france3-2018b]. The AngouLim association says it has been campaigning since 2018 for the line to be restored[^ici-2025-12]. In January 2024, a petition launched on behalf of the group asked the State and the Region to commit the funding and to reopen the line before 2029[^petition].

On 5 September 2025, about a hundred people, according to the association, gathered outside the sub-prefecture in Rochechouart. They asked for the study to be presented in public, for a funding agreement between the State and the Region with yearly instalments, for upkeep of the Haute-Vienne section and for a monitoring committee meeting every quarter[^ici-2025-09].

At the end of November 2025, the president of the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region, Alain Rousset, said the Region could not fund the reopening, and not within the next four or five years[^france3-2025]. On 5 December 2025, AngouLim met the regional president and the prefect of the Charente: the preliminary study is to be passed on to the association and to local councillors, but nothing has moved on funding[^ici-2025-12]. The association suggests finding the money elsewhere, through a tax on motorway profits, on aviation fuel or on lorries in transit, which it estimates would raise 20 to 40 million euros a year[^ici-2025-12].

## Where things stand

As of 22 November 2026, we have found no public decision that sets a date for works or a complete funding plan. Trains run from Limoges to Saillat-Chassenon; between Saillat and Angoulême, coaches provide the link.
